[size=4][b]NEW IN v1.2.1[/b][/size]

v1.2.1 is a text-only correction of four descriptions that did not match what MIR actually does - see the changelog. Nothing it does changed, and the Lua is the same as v1.2.0 apart from the version in its startup banner.

[size=4][b]NEW IN v1.2.0[/b][/size]
[list]
[*][b]A mod's own bag or chest is delivery, not placement.[/b] MIR used to assume that any container a mod shipped was placed somewhere in the world, and held everything inside it out of the pool so you could never find a duplicate. It cannot actually see where a container is placed - that lives in level data - and most mods simply hand you a backpack. On my own load order the old rule held back 5,575 items; the new one holds back 659 and gives 4,927 back. The trade is that an item a mod hands you can now also be found as MIR loot.
[*][b]One list, yours to change.[/b] The renamed [b]Exclusions and overrides[/b] tab opens with [b]Mods MIR does not use[/b]: every mod MIR skips, by name, ticked. Untick one - or delete it with the X - and MIR starts using it at the next rebuild. Mods you exclude yourself appear in the same list. Everything else on that tab now sits behind a [b]Show the advanced lists[/b] checkbox.
[*][b]Renamed: "Exclude items their mod already places in the world" is now "Excluded Mods",[/b] because the old name claimed something MIR cannot detect. Your setting carries over.
[*][b]Better reporting.[/b] !mir_tables gives the reason beside every held-back item and lists the ones MIR spawns anyway; !mir_placed lists prints MIR's own list with each row's state.
[/list]

[size=4][b]NEW IN v1.1.2[/b][/size]
[list]
[*][b]Fixed: items that are not scrolls were being placed as scrolls.[/b] Some mods set the game’s "this is a scroll" field on things that are not scrolls — a water cup and a modded injury kit, in my own load order — and MIR believed them, so those items appeared in bookshelves and desks. MIR now wants a second piece of evidence before it calls something a scroll, and the same 111 vanilla scrolls are recognised as before.
[*][b]New: Extra items (chests only).[/b] MIR catalogues equipment plus the consumables it recognises — potions, arrows, ingredients, scrolls — and skips everything else, which in a big load order is over a thousand objects. If a mod adds something that fits none of those categories and you want it in your loot, name it in that list on the Exclusions and overrides tab, under [b]Show the advanced lists[/b], and MIR will place it, [b]in chests only[/b]. Empty by default.
[/list]

[size=4][b]NEW IN v1.1 — loot sources[/b][/size]
[list]
[*][b]A new Loot Source Settings tab.[/b] Every kind of container has its own section — chests, corpses, barrels and crates, vases / urns / jars / amphorae, wardrobes, and bookshelves — each with its own on/off switch, including a new [b]Spawn in chests[/b] switch.
[*][b]Vases, urns, jars and amphorae are their own loot source,[/b] separate from barrels and crates, and off by default.
[*][b]Tick what you want.[/b] Barrels and vases each offer rings, potions, arrows, alchemy ingredients, or everything. Wardrobes offer cosmetic clothes, clothing and armour with stats, or everything. [b]Nothing ticked means nothing[/b] — there is no hidden "anything goes" fallback.
[*][b]Spawn chance and rolls per loot source.[/b] Tick [i]Use its own chance and rolls[/i] on any source to give it a different rate — for example corpses at 10% while chests stay at 25%. Sources you leave unticked use the General tab's chance and rolls.
[*][b]A chance or roll count of 0 no longer finishes containers for good.[/b] Anything passed over at 0 rolls once you raise it.
[*][b]Upgrading from v1.0.1?[/b] Vases start switched off, and wardrobes with all three boxes unticked now give nothing. Your other settings carry over. The CHANGELOG lists every change that affects an existing save.
[/list]

[line]

[b]Takes all of your modded equipment and seeds it into the world's loot, according to your specifications. This mod gives you clear, detailed controls in MCM over where modded items are placed — in treasure chests, on corpses, in wardrobes, in bookshelves, in barrels and in vases, each chosen separately — at a rate you control. Wardrobes give you clothing, bookshelves give you scrolls, barrels give you potions and arrows, and you can have the mod give you more kinds of item in each container if you want. The mod is strictly additive: it never removes, replaces, moves or reprices anything.[/b]

If you run dozens of equipment mods, you probably never see most of them. They never surface, because modded gear normally arrives through the tutorial chest, the camp chest, a vendor, or a console command — not through play. MIR hooks the moment loot appears: open a chest, a wardrobe or a bookshelf, or loot a corpse, for the first time, and MIR rolls. On a hit, one item from your modded pool is waiting in the loot panel on that first open. The game's own loot is untouched.

[b]Why another random equipment mod?[/b] I have used other equipment randomization mods and wanted one more suited to my personal preference. [b]Your preferences may vary, so I am not saying this mod is better than others — only different.[/b]

[b]The major difference:[/b] this mod is strictly for adding modded items to the game through gameplay, according to your preferences.

[b]No sidecar to run.[/b] MIR applies itself at runtime; a single pak file is all that is needed. It auto-indexes all your modded items and adds them as you loot.

[b]Designed for fine-tuning, transparency and ease of use.[/b] You can exclude specific items and mods, and every control in MCM is detailed and, I hope, easy to understand.

[size=4][b]How to use — please read[/b][/size]
[b]MIR REQUIRES MOD CONFIGURATION MENU (MCM). IF MCM IS NOT IN YOUR LOAD ORDER, MIR DISABLES ITSELF AND ADDS NOTHING TO THE GAME.[/b] It tells you so with a small in-game window and in its log. Load MCM [b]above[/b] MIR.
[list]
[*][b]Leave the defaults for a session and just play.[/b] One roll at 25% averages one spawn per four containers MIR actually rolls on — and a good share of what you open (barrels and vases while they are off, owned containers, carried bags, camp chests) is never rolled at all. You are not meant to notice a firehose.
[*][b]Open MCM[/b] (INSERT, or from the ESC menu) and find MIR: five tabs — General, Loot Source Settings, Pool, Rarity, Exclusions and overrides — plus the custom [b]MIR Browser[/b] tab, where you tune category shares and exclude mods or items.
[*][b]Every category row on the MIR Browser shows ON, LIMITED or OFF and says why.[/b] Weight 0 is the only way to switch a category off there; a category that can only appear in one loot source is switched off on that source's settings.
[*][b]Want barrels, crates or vases too?[/b] On the [i]Loot Source Settings[/i] tab, tick [i]Spawn in barrels, crates and other clutter[/i] or [i]Spawn in vases, urns, jars and amphorae[/i]. Each gives only what you tick — rings, potions, arrows, alchemy ingredients or everything — and the consumable boxes admit the game's own potions, arrows and ingredients there on their own, and only there.
[*][b]Want a different rate for one kind of container?[/b] Every loot source has [i]Use its own chance and rolls[/i]. Tick it and that source's own two sliders appear.
[/list]

[size=4][b]Features[/b][/size]
[list]
[*][b]Modded loot where you actually find loot[/b] — treasure chests, strongboxes, coffers, sarcophagi and the like, and every corpse you loot for the first time. Pre-placed bodies count; no combat required.
[*][b]Wardrobes give clothing.[/b] Their own loot source, on by default, filled with cosmetic clothes and stat-bearing armour rather than greatswords — or everything, if you tick that. With nothing ticked a wardrobe gives nothing. Your camp wardrobe is never touched.
[*][b]Bookshelves give scrolls.[/b] Bookcases, book rows, stacks and piles, scroll shelves and desks are their own type, on by default, holding scrolls — vanilla and modded — with an option to add everything else.
[*][b]Barrels and crates give what you tick.[/b] Off by default. Barrels, crates and every other container that is not a chest, vase, wardrobe or bookshelf can give rings, potions, arrows, alchemy ingredients, or everything. The consumable boxes admit vanilla and modded consumables there, and those items never leak into chests.
[*][b]Vases, urns, jars and amphorae are their own loot source[/b], off by default, with the same boxes. One detail worth knowing: a vase you walked past while vases were switched off is not lost. MIR stamps it as "skipped because vases were off", which is a retryable mark, so it becomes eligible the first time you open it after turning vases on. The same goes for barrels.
[*][b]A two-stage roll you can tune.[/b] How often (chance and rolls, set once or per loot source) is separate from what (a 0–100 slider per category). The MIR Browser shows each category's live percentage per container type.
[*][b]Equipment never drops twice in the same save[/b] — a per-save ledger takes it out of the pool for good. Consumables (potions, arrows, ingredients, scrolls) are exempt and can repeat.
[*][b]Exclusion browser.[/b] Every mod contributing items, sorted by count; drill into a mod and tick out a whole mod or a single item. Applies immediately, persists per MCM profile.
[*][b]Gear a mod puts on a specific NPC stays out of the pool[/b] by default - it belongs to that character. A mod's own backpack, bag or chest does NOT count: that is the mod handing the items to you rather than placing them in the world, so those items stay in MIR's pool and you may end up with two - the mod's copy and MIR's. MIR also ships a short list of mods and containers that really are placed in the world and skips those; one tick per mod changes it.
[*][b]Known utility mods are fenced out[/b] by default — photo-mode rings, cheat spawners, framework libraries.
[*][b]NPC and companion gear is PERMANENTLY excluded — there is no toggle.[/b] Starting-gear upgrades, NPC redesigns and evolutions, companion looks mods, join-the-party companion mods. That gear belongs to its character; finding Astarion's bespoke coat in a goblin's footlocker before you meet him is not a feature. Ask and more mods can be added to the list.
[*][b]An optional rarity window[/b] limits which rarities can spawn by your character level and by Act.
[*][b]Nothing is written into your save by MIR's settings.[/b] They live in MCM's per-profile store. Safe to install or remove mid-playthrough.
[/list]

[size=4][b]Requirements[/b][/size]
[list]
[*]Baldur's Gate 3 — developed and tested on Patch 8.
[*][b]BG3 Script Extender[/b] (version 29+). Required — MIR is entirely Script Extender Lua.
[*][b]Mod Configuration Menu (BG3MCM)[/b] — [b]a hard requirement, enforced at runtime.[/b] Developed and tested against v1.40.1. Load it [b]above[/b] MIR. If MCM is not in your load order, MIR builds no pool, rolls on nothing and injects nothing, and says so in game and in its log ([b]!mir_status[/b] reads [b]mcm=MISSING - MIR DORMANT[/b]).
[*]A mod manager (BG3 Mod Manager recommended) — MIR must be [b]active in your load order[/b].
[*][b]Windows only[/b] (Script Extender is Windows-only). MCM's configuration window is driven with [b]mouse and keyboard[/b].
[/list]

[size=4][b]Install / uninstall[/b][/size]
[list]
[*]Drop [b]MIR.pak[/b] into [b]%LocalAppData%\Larian Studios\Baldur's Gate 3\Mods[/b], activate it in BG3MM, put [b]Mod Configuration Menu above MIR[/b], save the load order, launch.
[*][b]Verify:[/b] the Script Extender console prints a [b]MIR v1.1.1 ... loaded[/b] banner; type [b]server[/b] then [b]!mir_status[/b] and look for [b]catalogBuilt=true[/b] and [b]mcm=detected[/b].
[*][b]Uninstall:[/b] deactivate and delete the pak. [b]Safe mid-save[/b] — MIR only ever added things; already-spawned items are ordinary items from mods you still have, and they simply stay where they are.
[*][b]Mid-playthrough install is fine.[/b] MIR only sees containers and corpses you interact with after installing. A chest you emptied last week is unrolled as far as MIR is concerned, so it rolls the next time you open it.
[/list]
Full instructions, a settings reference and a console reference are in the README and INSTALL files in the download (Markdown, Word and PDF).

[size=4][b]Configuration — five MCM tabs, plus the MIR Browser[/b][/size]
[list]
[*][b]General[/b] — master switch, the spawn chance and rolls every loot source uses unless it has its own, the Nautiloid exclusion, the item-received notification.
[*][b]Loot Source Settings[/b] — one section each for chests, corpses, barrels and crates, vases/urns/jars/amphorae, wardrobes and bookshelves: the source's on/off switch, what it may give (barrels and vases: rings / potions / arrows / alchemy ingredients / everything; wardrobes: cosmetic clothes / clothing and armour with stats / everything; bookshelves: scrolls / also all other items), and [i]Use its own chance and rolls[/i], which reveals that source's own two sliders. [b]Nothing ticked means nothing[/b] — there is no unrestricted fallback.
[*][b]Pool[/b] — include base-game items, cosmetics, modded consumables, base-game consumables, [b]scrolls[/b] (one switch for vanilla and modded together; bookshelves, and barrels or vases on everything, get scrolls regardless); the utility fence; the already-placed-in-the-world filter.
[*][b]Rarity[/b] — optional, off by default. Limit which rarities can spawn by character level (bands 1–5, 6–10, 11–15, 16–20+) and by Act, choosing a lowest and highest rarity for each; the MOST RESTRICTIVE of the two rules applies. Alchemy ingredients ignore the window (the game gives them no rarity, so they would all vanish under one).
[*][b]Exclusions and overrides[/b] - one list of the mods and containers MIR does not use, which you change with a tick, plus a checkbox that reveals five more: three per-item lists (the first of them normally managed from the MIR Browser tab, which knows every item's name) and read-only views of the utility fence, which follows its own toggle on the Pool tab, and the NPC-gear fence, which has no toggle at all.
[*][b]MIR Browser[/b] (custom tab) — the only place category shares are edited: 17 categories (weapons, shields, body armour, helmets, gloves, boots, cloaks, rings, amulets, camp clothing, camp shoes, underwear, potions and elixirs, arrows, alchemy ingredients, scrolls, extra items), each with a derived [b]ON / LIMITED / OFF[/b] status, a live percentage per container type, and a 0–100 weight slider. Plus the searchable, paged exclusion browser.
[/list]
Every change applies live — no restart, no reload. Settings persist [b]per MCM profile, globally across saves[/b] — never inside a savegame.

[size=4][b]Compatibility[/b][/size]
[b]Compatible:[/b] equipment/armour/clothing mods of every kind (they are the raw material), mods that alter vendor stock (MIR doesn't touch traders), mods that add containers or corpses.
Also expected to coexist, on code-level analysis rather than a joint playtest: [b]Container Loot Control[/b] (Volitio) and [b]Randomized Item Framework[/b] (GraphicFade). [b]Do not combine Randomized Item Framework with MIR's "Include base-game items"[/b] — that puts both mods on the same items.

[b]NOT compatible — do not run these alongside MIR:[/b]
[list]
[*][b]Randomized Equipment Loot[/b] and its family, [b]Fade's Equipment Distribution AIO's randomizer feature[/b], and any other mod that randomizes or re-places loot [b]by rewriting loot data[/b]. Two of these together give compounded spawn rates at best and contradictory loot tables at worst. Pick one.
[/list]

[b]Caveats:[/b]
[list]
[*][b]Auto-loot / loot-vacuum mods may bypass MIR.[/b] MIR rolls on the engine events fired when a player opens a container or first loots a corpse; a mod that empties containers at a distance may not fire them. First thing to check if you never see spawns.
[*][b]Remote container access[/b] (remote camp-chest commands and the like) bypasses MIR entirely. By design.
[*][b]Quest mods:[/b] items placed on a specific NPC or in a specific world container are kept out by default and StoryItem/IsKey templates are always excluded, but items a quest mod spawns from its own script can't be detected — exclude such a mod by hand if you want certainty.
[*][b]Multiplayer:[/b] every player needs MIR, Script Extender and MCM on the same versions as the host (the game compares mod lists when a player joins, and it cannot download a Nexus mod for you). MIR is server-authoritative: its loot logic runs only on the host, so each container is rolled once and every player sees the same items, and [b]the host manages the settings[/b]. Non-host settings changes are refused. Not yet tested in a live multiplayer session; reports welcome.
[/list]

[size=4][b]Known limitations[/b][/size]
[list]
[*][b]MCM is mandatory.[/b] There is no defaults-only mode. The in-game notice is a Script Extender window, so where Script Extender's UI layer is broken the only notice is the banner in [b]MIR_log.txt[/b].
[*][b]Base-game duplicates are expected if you turn "Include base-game items" on[/b] — the game places vanilla items by hand, on level data MIR cannot see, so MIR cannot know one is already waiting for you. That is why the setting defaults to off. Modded items are different: MIR can see when a mod places its own item and keeps those out by default; [b]!mir_placed_mods[/b] names the mods that place their own items.
[*][b]You may find two of something[/b] - since v1.2.0 a mod's own bag or chest counts as delivery, not placement, so those items are in MIR's pool while the mod still gives you its copy. On a 1,610-module load order that is 4,927 items back in the pool and 659 still held back; before v1.2.0 the same order lost 5,575 to the filter. !mir_tables names every item in both groups, and a mod you would rather MIR left alone is one tick on the Exclusions and overrides tab.
[*][b]Late-game rate decline[/b] — equipment never repeats, so categories empty as a playthrough goes on. Add more mods or raise the base chance.
[*][b]Reloading re-rolls.[/b] No deterministic seed: reload a save from before a container was opened and it rolls again.
[*][b]Container classification is by name.[/b] Shields are detected by the offhand slot (a mod's offhand weapon can be labelled a shield); apothecary and tailor desks count as desks; one wine rack is shaped like a scroll shelf. Modded scroll detection is a heuristic.
[*][b]MCM quirks:[/b] the read-only exclusion views still render clickable checkboxes (clicking does nothing), and MCM's list widgets may need a tab reopen to show exclusions written from the browser.
[/list]
